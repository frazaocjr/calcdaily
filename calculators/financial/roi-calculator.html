How Return on Investment Is Calculated

The calculator subtracts the initial investment from the amount returned to estimate net profit. It then divides that profit by the initial investment and expresses the result as a percentage. A positive percentage indicates that the entered return is above the entered cost; a negative percentage indicates a loss on those inputs.

What ROI does not tell you

This simple ratio does not account for how long the investment was held, cash-flow timing, risk, taxes, inflation, financing costs, or opportunity cost. Two projects with the same ROI can have very different risk and time profiles.

Financial note: ROI is one comparison measure, not a complete investment analysis or personal financial recommendation.

Example and limitations

A positive ROI means the amount returned exceeds the initial investment; a negative ROI indicates a loss. The calculation is a simple comparison and does not annualize returns or adjust for timing.

How is ROI different from investment growth?

Simple ROI compares a return or ending value with the initial amount invested and expresses the gain or loss as a percentage. It does not account for the time required to earn that return, compound growth, taxes or other investment risks; use an investment projection for contribution-based scenarios.

Scope: This simple ROI comparison does not account for cash-flow timing, financing costs, taxes, inflation, or risk. Compare like-for-like periods before using it to assess alternatives.
