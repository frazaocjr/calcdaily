How the Voltage Drop Estimate Works

The calculator estimates conductor resistance from the selected AWG cross-sectional area and a standard material resistivity at approximately 20°C. It then applies a two-conductor factor for DC or single-phase circuits, or a square-root-of-three factor for a balanced three-phase estimate.

Formula and method

Conductor resistance ≈ resistivity × one-way length ÷ conductor area. DC / single-phase drop ≈ 2 × current × resistance. Balanced three-phase drop ≈ √3 × current × resistance. Percentage drop = voltage drop ÷ system voltage × 100.

Tips for better estimates

Use the actual one-way route length, not the round-trip cable length. Select the correct material, AWG, circuit type, and nominal voltage for an initial comparison.

Important limitations

This is a simplified resistive estimate and is not an electrical design or safety approval. Temperature, conductor insulation, installation method, reactance, terminals, code requirements, and protective devices are excluded. A qualified electrician must verify sizing.

Example and limitations

Voltage-drop estimates depend on conductor temperature, installation method, connections, and applicable electrical codes. A qualified electrician or engineer must verify conductor sizing and safety before installation.

What affects voltage drop in a wire?

A wire's resistance varies with length, cross-section, material and temperature. Current and circuit configuration also matter. This is a simplified resistive estimate, not a substitute for electrical design, conductor ampacity checks or local-code compliance.
