How the Ovulation Window Is Estimated

The calculator uses the entered last-period date and cycle length. It estimates ovulation by assuming a luteal phase of about 14 days, then displays a fertile window from five days before the estimated ovulation date through the following day. The method is a calendar projection based on the cycle length supplied.

Calendar predictions cannot confirm ovulation

Ovulation timing can vary between people and from one cycle to another. Stress, illness, travel, postpartum changes, and irregular cycles can make calendar estimates less reliable. Ovulation predictor tests and clinical assessment may provide additional information.

Health note: This calculator cannot confirm fertility, diagnose an ovulation disorder, or prevent pregnancy. Do not use its dates as a contraceptive method.

Example and limitations

Ovulation does not always occur on the same cycle day, and a calendar window cannot confirm ovulation or prevent pregnancy. Consider a qualified healthcare professional for contraception or fertility concerns.

Can a calendar predict ovulation exactly?

No. The displayed ovulation date and fertile window are based on average cycle timing and may differ substantially from an individual's actual cycle. Calendar estimates should not be relied on to prevent pregnancy or to diagnose fertility concerns.

Source and important context

The NHS notes that ovulation timing varies between people and cycles. A calendar estimate cannot confirm ovulation or reliably identify days when pregnancy cannot occur.
