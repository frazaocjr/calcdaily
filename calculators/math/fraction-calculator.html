How Fraction Arithmetic Works

For addition and subtraction, the calculator uses a common denominator before combining numerators. For multiplication, it multiplies the numerators and denominators. For division, it multiplies by the reciprocal of the second fraction. It then reduces the resulting fraction using the greatest common divisor and displays a decimal equivalent.

Check denominators

A denominator cannot be zero, and division by a fraction with a zero numerator is undefined. Mixed numbers should be converted correctly to numerator and denominator before entering them.

Example and limitations

For example, 1/4 + 1/2 is evaluated by converting both fractions to a common denominator, giving 3/4. A decimal display may be rounded for presentation, while the simplified fraction remains the exact result when supported.

How to Add, Subtract, Multiply, and Divide Fractions

To add 1/4 + 1/2, rewrite 1/2 as 2/4, then add the numerators: 1/4 + 2/4 = 3/4. To subtract, use a common denominator and subtract numerators. To multiply, multiply the numerators and the denominators. To divide, multiply by the reciprocal of the second fraction, provided it is not zero. This tool accepts two fractions and shows a decimal equivalent of the result; it is not a standalone decimal-to-fraction or mixed-number converter.
